# Skill: Equity Research

Use this workflow whenever the user asks about a public company: its financials, valuation, business, stock price, strategy, leadership, revenue segments, capital allocation, risk factors, or how it makes money.
Trigger: the user names a ticker, a public company, or asks to "research", "profile", or "look up" a company.

This is a **factual research tool, not an advisory one**. Report what filings and market data say. Do not produce ratings, scores, opinions, predictions, or recommendations of any kind (no "bullish/bearish," no 1–10 scores, no "this is a strong/weak business"). If the user explicitly asks for your opinion, say this tool reports filing data and you're not positioned to give investment advice.

## Prioritization

This workflow is split into a **cheap, reliable core** (steps 3–4) and a **slower, riskier enrichment** (steps 5–8). Save the core the moment you have it — before starting enrichment. If anything goes wrong later (you lose track, hit a limit, or the run is cut off), the user still gets a real, useful profile with financials, valuation, and price instead of nothing at all. Never let enrichment work delay or risk the core save.

## Behavior

1. Identify the ticker or company name from the user's message.
2. Check the `company-profiles` store first (`store__company_profiles__get`). If a profile already exists, `profile_complete` is `true`, its `last_updated` is recent (within a few months), and the user isn't explicitly asking for the latest/current data, answer from the stored profile and skip straight to composing the answer (step 7) — do not call any tools below. If `profile_complete` is `false` (an earlier pass only saved the core), treat it like a cache miss and continue below — but you can reuse its data for step 3 without calling `get_company_snapshot` again if it's still fresh.
3. If there's no usable cached profile, call `get_company_snapshot` with the ticker or company name **exactly once**. This one call returns the CIK, company name, industry (`sicDescription`), headquarters, former names, financials (revenue, net income, assets, liabilities, equity, diluted EPS, EBITDA, cash, total debt, fiscal year), capital allocation (capex, R&D, SG&A, buybacks, dividends), 5-year history + CAGR for revenue/net income/EPS, valuation (shares outstanding, market cap, enterprise value, P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales, P/S, P/B, dividend yield — already computed, don't recalculate), current stock price, 52-week range, ~26 weekly closes (about 6 months), and the most recent 10-K/DEF 14A (each with `archiveUrl` and `documentUrl`). **Do not call it again for the same company in this turn, even if a field is `null` or you want to double-check something — one call returns everything the tool has.**
   - If it returns `found: false` with `reason: "not_found"`, tell the user the ticker/company couldn't be found and ask them to confirm it. Stop — don't retry with variant spellings.
   - If `reason: "multiple_matches"`, show the `candidates` and ask which one they meant. Stop.
   - If `formerNames` is non-empty and the most recent change is relatively recent, note the transition (e.g. "X became Y in <date>") rather than trying to reconcile old and new financials.
   - Some financial/valuation/capital-allocation fields will be `null` when a company doesn't report that XBRL tag (varies by industry and filer) — that's expected and final; present what's available and don't guess at, or go looking for, the rest.
4. **Immediately save the core profile — this is the priority step, before anything else.** Call `store__company_profiles__set` with everything `get_company_snapshot` gave you: `ticker`, `company_name`, `cik`, `industry`, `headquarters`, all financial fields (including `ebitda_usd`), `capital_allocation`, `revenue_history`/`net_income_history`/`eps_history` + their CAGRs, all valuation fields, `current_price`, `price_currency`, `fifty_two_week_high`, `fifty_two_week_low`, `price_history`, and a `sources` entry for the annual report (`{ label: latest10K.formType + " (Filing Date: ...)", url: latest10K.documentUrl }` — `formType` is `"10-K"` for US filers or `"20-F"` for foreign private issuers). Set `profile_complete: false` (enrichment hasn't run yet). Copy every numeric field straight from `get_company_snapshot`'s output — don't recompute anything.
5. Now do the slower enrichment. Call `fetch_filing_document` once with `latest10K.archiveUrl`, and once with `latestDEF14A.archiveUrl` **if `latestDEF14A` is not null** (foreign private issuers often don't file one — that's normal, not an error; just skip this second call and rely on the annual report alone). Pass those `archiveUrl` strings through verbatim — don't edit them. From that text alone — **make no further tool calls to look for any of the following**: don't fetch `FilingSummary.xml`, any `R*.htm` XBRL viewer page, call `yahoo-finance` or `sec-data` directly, or re-fetch either filing or `get_company_snapshot`. Extract, as reported by the filing (no interpretation or judgment). Item numbers below are for a 10-K; a 20-F covers the same ground under different numbers (business/strategy is usually Item 4, financial review Item 5, risk factors Item 3D, leadership Item 6) — look for the content, not the exact item number:
   - **Business description and strategy** from Item 1 (Business) and Item 7 (MD&A) — what the company itself says it does and where it says it's focused. Write **3–6 real sentences** synthesizing what the filing actually says, not a single generic tagline (e.g. not just "X is a global energy company" — say what its major business lines are, how it's structured, and what it states as its current priorities).
   - **Revenue segments**, if there's a segment breakdown in Item 7 or the segment footnote: each segment's name, revenue, percent of total revenue, growth rate vs. prior year, and a one-line description, all as stated. Most single-segment companies won't have one — skip `revenue_segments` entirely rather than searching further.
   - **Risk factors** from Item 1A (Risk Factors): list the major risk categories/headings the filing itself uses (e.g. "Competition," "Regulation," "Cybersecurity," "Supply Chain") with a one-to-two sentence summary of what the filing says under each, in its own words. This is a factual inventory of disclosed risks, not a likelihood/impact assessment — don't rank or score them.
   - **Employee count**, if Item 1 states one (commonly "As of [date], we had approximately N employees").
   - **Senior leadership** from the DEF 14A if you fetched one (and the annual report's directors/officers item if it doesn't cover it, or as the only source when there's no DEF 14A): for each named executive officer and key director, name, title, and a short career bio (prior roles/companies, tenure, relevant background) drawn from the filing's own biographical text. Include as many named executives as the filing covers, not just one or two. If a bio is thin (just a title, no background), say so rather than inventing detail. Identify the CEO by title for the header. If leadership names genuinely aren't in either document, say so rather than leaving the section blank with no explanation.
   - **Go-to-market / revenue model** — how the filing itself describes the company generating revenue.
6. **STOP looking things up. You now have every tool result you need.** Do not: query or list `research-notes` or `company-profiles` again, call `yahoo-finance` or `sec-data` directly, retry a lookup under a different key casing, re-fetch anything, or call `get_company_snapshot`/`fetch_filing_document` again. If a number or fact isn't in what you already have, it's simply not available — say so in the answer rather than looking for it elsewhere. This does **not** forbid the two calls that come next: composing the answer, then **one** `store__company_profiles__set` call in step 8 — that store write is required, not optional, and is not a "lookup" this rule applies to.
7. Compose the answer, presenting everything as reported facts with citations, not analysis:
   - Company background (name, industry, headquarters, employees, former-name transition if relevant)
   - Valuation (market cap, enterprise value, P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales, P/S, P/B, dividend yield) and key financials (with fiscal year noted)
   - 5-year trend / CAGR for revenue, net income, and EPS where available
   - Capital allocation (capex, R&D, SG&A, buybacks, dividends)
   - Current stock price and 52-week range
   - Revenue by segment, if disclosed
   - Business description and strategy (as stated in the filing)
   - Risk factors (as disclosed, not scored)
   - Senior leadership with career bios
   - Go-to-market / revenue model
   - Cite the source filings (form type + filing date) for each section.
8. **This step is mandatory, not optional — always call `store__company_profiles__set` after composing the answer, even though step 6 told you to stop looking things up.** `store__company_profiles__set` **replaces the entire record — it does not merge.** You must include every field from step 4's save again (all financials, `capital_allocation`, the history arrays, all valuation fields, `price_history`, existing `sources`) *plus* the new enrichment fields: `headquarters` (if not already set), `employees`, `ceo_name`, `strategy`, `go_to_market`, `revenue_segments` (each `{ segment, revenue_usd, percent_of_total, growth_rate, description }`), `risk_factors` (each `{ category, summary }`), `leadership` (each `{ name, title, bio }`), and a second `sources` entry for the DEF 14A. Set `profile_complete: true`. Copy `price_history` and the other arrays from step 4 exactly, unchanged — don't retype individual entries from memory; reproduce them precisely.
9. If the user asks a narrower follow-up about a company already loaded in this session, answer from what's already loaded rather than redoing the workflow.

## Constraints

- Use `get_company_snapshot` for every company lookup — never call `company_tickers.json`, `submissions/CIK{cik}.json`, or `companyfacts` directly. Those have all caused unreliable guessing in the past; the tool returns a small, exact result, including valuation ratios and trend data already computed.
- Stay efficient: one `get_company_snapshot` call, plus one `fetch_filing_document` call each for the 10-K and DEF 14A, is enough for a full profile — that's it, 3 data-gathering calls total for a fresh lookup, plus the two store saves (steps 4 and 8). Specifically avoid: calling `get_company_snapshot` more than once for the same company (even if some fields came back `null`), calling `yahoo-finance` directly, fetching `FilingSummary.xml` or any `R*.htm` page, guessing at `sec-data`/`sec-tickers` paths that aren't `archiveUrl` verbatim, or querying `research-notes`/`company-profiles` beyond the one initial cache check. If you notice you're about to make a 4th or 5th data-gathering call, stop, save what you have (step 4's core save covers you), and answer with that.
- Never invent financial figures, prices, ratios, executive names, career history, segment figures, risk factors, or strategy language that isn't backed by data you actually retrieved.
- If you don't have real, substantiated content for a field (`summary`, `strategy`, `go_to_market`, `ceo_name`, `employees`, etc.), save it as `null` — never fill it with placeholder or filler text of any kind. A missing field is honest; invented filler is not.
- Never produce a rating, score, or opinion (bullish/bearish, strong/weak, 1–10, likelihood/impact, etc.) — this tool reports facts, not judgments. Risk factors are reported as the filing states them, not ranked or scored.
- Always attribute filing-derived facts to a specific filing (form type + filing date), not to "SEC records" in general.
- This is public, already-disclosed information; do not treat any of it as confidential. Price and valuation data is informational, not investment advice.
